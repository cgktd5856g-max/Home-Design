# Next videos: 20 ranked ideas

Research date: 22 Nov 2026. Updated with Haven Haus's YouTube data and the 59-video in-depth comparison.
Evidence is in `report.md` (sections 4-6 and 13).

## What the data changed

- **Room priority**: bedrooms (1.51 times normal) and kids' rooms (1.35) first, then wardrobes (1.05) and
  kitchens (1.00, but the single biggest hit at 19.3M). Balcony, bathroom, entry and living rooms trail
  (0.70-0.92). In 2026 every hit was a bedroom or kids' room with a raised or loft bed.
- **A whole room, not one piece**: 28 of 29 hits build or transform a whole room; 18 of 30 flops show one
  cabinet, wall or corner. Ideas built around a single piece now rank lower, and each one builds the room around
  its star feature.
- **Never still**: hits change visibly in nearly half of all 0.2 s steps and rarely hold still for more than
  1.5 s, because their camera glides through every shot (21 of 25 pairs); flops hold locked-off views. Keep the
  camera drifting inside every station. Our kitchen holds still far more than any hit.
- **Clean colour**: hits are white or pale grey with light or mid wood, and the colour comes from what fills the
  storage. Flops drift into cream or orange casts, dark wood or saturated themes.
- **Length**: every Haven Haus hit is a 34-53 s multi-zone tour, and in the matched pairs the hit was longer 22
  times out of 25. Make each top idea in **two cuts**: the 15 s cut (your current pipeline) and a **long cut of
  40-50 s** that chains 3-4 zones. Compare them after 7 days.
- **People**: four of the top five show a person using a feature. Where the renderer allows, add a simple
  stand-in figure for one moment; otherwise make the feature visibly work (the step drawer pulls out, the table
  slides out).

## Rules for every idea

- Frame 1 is the empty room (or a wall breaking open), and the first landing comes by 0.3 s. No on-screen text.
- Build the whole room. When one feature is the star, build the room around it first, then reveal the feature.
- A sound on every landing over original music at about 115-125 BPM, where most hits sit (weak evidence); one or
  two playful effects. Cutting exactly on the beat is optional: the hits' scene changes don't line up with strong
  sounds.
- Palette: white or pale-grey shell, light or mid wood, and colour from what fills the storage (books, clothes,
  jars). No cream or orange cast, dark wood, saturated walls or busy patterns.
- Keep the camera moving: a slow push-in or orbit inside every station, so no moment holds still for more than
  about 1.5 s. Even the final hold drifts slowly instead of freezing.
- 15 s cut: 4 stations with 0.25-0.3 s swings at about 3.0, 7.0 and 10.7 s; wow at 8-11 s; lights at about
  12.2 s; return to the frame-1 viewpoint 12.5-15.0 s for the loop.
- Long cut: the same opening, then a new zone every 6-10 s, each with a build and a reveal, and a new angle every
  2-4 s; the wow at about 60-70% of the runtime; a 2 s wide at the end. No end card.
- Before posting, run `python3 research/hh_metrics.py <video>`: motion 7.5 or more, longest still 1.8 s or less,
  camera movement 1.8 or more, saturation 0.17-0.38, warmth 22-57, brightness 138-178.
- Packed with storage, a different layout every time, renders bright and clean.
- Caption: the size and the problem ("9 m², three kids"), plus a question that starts a debate ("Would you sleep
  on the top bunk?"). Pin a comment answering "which app?": rendered from code.

## How to learn from these

- Post 3-4 a week, natively on each platform. Buffer puts the sweet spot at 3-5 a week on Instagram and 2-5 on
  TikTok, and Haven Haus posted 5-6 a week at its peak.
- For ideas 1-5, post the long cut and the 15 s cut a few days apart, then compare engaged views and average
  watch time.
- After 7 days, add each video's numbers to `videos.csv` (`set` = `ours`), then run
  `python3 research/youtube_inventory.py stats research/videos.csv --where set=ours`.
